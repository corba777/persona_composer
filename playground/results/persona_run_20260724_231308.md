## Executive Summary

Fluid Catalytic Cracking (FCC) is the single most important conversion process in a refinery — it turns heavy vacuum gas oil (VGO) into gasoline, LPG, and light olefins. The catalyst is a microsphere (~70 μm) built from **zeolite Y** embedded in a matrix of alumina, silica, clay, and rare earth oxides. Choosing the right catalyst is a multi-variable optimization problem: labs run standardized tests (ACE, MAT, DCR) measuring conversion, gasoline yield, coke selectivity, bottoms cracking, octane, metals tolerance, and attrition resistance. The "best" catalyst depends on the specific crude slate, unit constraints, and product value targets — there is no universal winner. This makes it a perfect ML classification/ranking problem.

## 1. What Is an FCC Catalyst?

### The Process
```
                    ┌─────────────┐
  Heavy VGO ───────►│   RISER     │───► Products (gasoline, LPG, LCO, bottoms, coke)
  (350-550°C bp)    │  (~520°C)   │
                    │  ~3 seconds │
                    └──────┬──────┘
                           │ spent catalyst + coke
                    ┌──────▼──────┐
                    │ REGENERATOR │───► Flue gas (CO₂)
                    │  (~700°C)   │
                    │  burn coke  │
                    └──────┬──────┘
                           │ regenerated catalyst
                           └──────► back to riser
```

The catalyst circulates at **~5 tonnes/minute** in a large unit. It's consumed — you add **1-10 tonnes/day** of fresh catalyst (makeup) to replace losses and maintain activity.

### Catalyst Composition

| Component | wt% | Role |
|-----------|-----|------|
| **Zeolite Y** (USY, REUSY) | 15-40% | Primary cracking component. Provides micropores (~7.4 Å supercages). Drives conversion and gasoline selectivity |
| **Active Matrix** (alumina) | 10-30% | Pre-cracks large molecules that can't enter zeolite pores. Critical for bottoms cracking and resid processing |
| **Clay** (kaolin) | 20-40% | Filler, structural integrity, heat capacity, cheap |
| **Binder** (silica sol, alumina sol) | 5-15% | Holds the microsphere together, attrition resistance |
| **Rare Earth Oxides** (La₂O₃, Ce₂O₃) | 1-5% | Stabilize zeolite framework, increase activity, BUT reduce octane |
| **Additives** | separate particles | ZSM-5 (octane/propylene boost), Pt-based CO promoter, SOₓ/NOₓ reduction, Ni/V traps |

### Key Zeolite Parameters

- **Unit Cell Size (UCS):** Fresh zeolite ~24.50 Å, equilibrium ~24.24-24.35 Å. Lower UCS = more dealuminated = higher octane, lower activity
- **Rare Earth level:** More RE → higher activity, hydrogen transfer → lower octane, more coke
- **Surface Area:** Zeolite SA (micropore) + Matrix SA (mesopore). Total fresh ~300-350 m²/g, equilibrium ~100-180 m²/g
- **Crystallinity:** % of zeolite structure intact after steaming/metals deactivation

## 2. How Labs Evaluate FCC Catalysts

### Standardized Lab Tests

| Test | Full Name | Description |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| **MAT** | Micro-Activity Test | ASTM D3907. Fixed bed, C/O ~3, 482°C. Gives conversion %. Older, less realistic |
| **ACE** | Advanced Cracking Evaluation | Fixed fluidized bed. Multiple C/O ratios (3-9). More realistic yields. Industry standard since ~2010 |
| **DCR** | Davison Circulating Riser | Pilot-scale circulating unit. Most realistic but expensive. Grace/W.R. Grace proprietary |
| **SCT-RT** | Short Contact Time Resid Test | Similar to DCR but for resid feeds |

### What Gets Measured (the Y-variables for your ML model)

**Primary Performance Metrics:**
1. **Conversion (wt%)** — (Feed - LCO - Bottoms - Coke) / Feed × 100, or 100 - LCO - Bottoms
2. **Gasoline yield (wt%)** — C₅-221°C fraction
3. **LPG yield (wt%)** — C₃ + C₄
4. **Bottoms yield (wt%)** — 343°C+ fraction (lower is better)
5. **Coke yield (wt%)** — burned off in regenerator
6. **Dry Gas yield (wt%)** — H₂ + C₁ + C₂ (lower is better — thermal cracking indicator)

**Selectivity Metrics (the real differentiators):**
7. **Coke selectivity** — coke/conversion at constant conversion (interpolated)
8. **Bottoms cracking** — bottoms at constant conversion
9. **Gasoline selectivity** — gasoline/conversion
10. **LPG olefinicity** — propylene/total C₃, butylenes/total C₄

**Quality Metrics:**
11. **RON / MON** — Research/Motor Octane Number of gasoline fraction
12. **Gasoline olefins, aromatics, sulfur content**

**Physical Properties:**
13. **Attrition Index (AI)** — ASTM D5757, measures fines generation
14. **Apparent Bulk Density (ABD)**
15. **Particle Size Distribution (PSD)** — 0-40μ fines, average particle size
16. **Total Surface Area, Zeolite SA, Matrix SA**

**Metals Tolerance (for resid operations):**
17. **Ni on Ecat, V on Ecat** — metals accumulation
18. **H₂ yield at metals** — nickel dehydrogenation activity
19. **V-induced zeolite destruction** — SA retention at high V

### Deactivation Protocols

Before testing, fresh catalyst is **artificially deactivated** to simulate equilibrium catalyst (Ecat):

| Method | Conditions | Use |
|--------|-----------|-----|
| **CPS** (Cyclic Propylene Steaming) | 788°C, steam + propylene cycles, 20h | Simulates metals + hydrothermal. Industry standard |
| **Mitchell Impregnation + Steam** | Ni/V impregnated, then 788°C steam 4-20h | Older method |
| **CDU** (Cyclic Deactivation Unit) | Repeated crack/regen cycles with metals-doped feed | Most realistic, most expensive |

```

Step 3: Lab evaluation (4-12 weeks)
        ├── Deactivate fresh samples (CPS or steam)
        ├── Run ACE tests at multiple C/O ratios
        ├── Compare yield structures at CONSTANT CONVERSION (critical!)
        ├── Measure physical properties
        └── Sometimes: pilot plant trial

Step 4: Technical committee review
        ├── Plot yield vs. conversion curves
        ├── Calculate economic value ($ per barrel) from yield shifts
        ├── Assess risk (new technology? proven?)
        └── Negotiate price

Step 5: Plant trial (3-6 months)
        └── Gradually change over catalyst inventory
```

### Why It's Hard

The core difficulty: **you're optimizing ~10 outputs simultaneously with ~20 input variables, and the objectives conflict.**

- More rare earth → more conversion → more coke → regen limited
- More matrix activity → better bottoms cracking → maybe more coke
- Lower UCS → higher octane → lower activity → need more catalyst addition
- ZSM-5 additive → more propylene → less gasoline → maybe that's good?

Refineries typically use **interpolation at constant conversion** and **LP (linear programming) models** to estimate the economic value of yield shifts. A shift of 0.5 wt% gasoline at the expense of 0.3 wt% coke could be worth $0.30-1.00/bbl depending on margins.

## 4. ML Approaches to FCC Catalyst Selection

### What's Been Done in Literature

| Approach | Details |
|----------|---------|
| **ANN for yield prediction** | Neural networks predicting conversion, gasoline, coke from catalyst properties + operating conditions. Common in academic literature since ~2005 |
| **Random Forest / XGBoost** | Tabular data → yield prediction. Works well with small datasets (100-500 experiments) |
| **Gaussian Process Regression** | Good for uncertainty quantification — tells you "how confident" the prediction is |
| **Bayesian Optimization** | For active learning: suggest the NEXT experiment to run |
| **Multi-objective optimization** | Pareto front of gasoline vs. coke vs. bottoms. NSGA-II common |

### Recommended Architecture for Your Problem

Since you want to **rank catalysts for a given experiment** (i.e., given feed properties and unit conditions, find the top 3 catalysts), this is a **Learning-to-Rank** or **multi-output regression + ranking** problem.
